Chiang Mai · Field Notes

Eight stops worth the drive.

Not a top-ten list copied from a search engine — this is what your driver actually takes people to see, written down properly for once. Mix a few of these into a day, or let him build the route.

Most trips start the same way — a pickup, a name on a sign, and a long list of places to get to.

None of this is sponsored and nothing here is ranked. It's roughly grouped by what kind of day you're after: temple and views, water and waterfalls, wildlife, and the two stops people ask for once they've already seen the obvious ones.

Temple · 15 min from the Old City

Wat Phra That Doi Suthep

The gold chedi on the mountain that watches over the whole city. Founded in the 14th century, the story goes that a white elephant carrying a sacred relic climbed the mountain, trumpeted three times, and died on the spot — the temple was built where it fell. You reach the top by a 300-odd step naga staircase, or a short funicular if the stairs aren't the point of the trip.

💡Good to know: shoulders and knees covered to enter the temple grounds — sarongs are usually available to borrow at the entrance if you forget.
Mountain · ~1.5–2 hrs from the city

Doi Inthanon National Park

2,565 metres up — the highest point in Thailand, and noticeably cooler than the city below. The drive climbs through hill-tribe villages and strawberry farms before reaching the twin royal chedis near the summit, built for the King and Queen's 60th birthdays. There's usually a coffee stop somewhere on the way up; take it.

💡Good to know: the summit can be genuinely cold — bring a layer, even in the hot season.
Waterfall · en route to Doi Inthanon

Wachirathan Waterfall

A short walk from the road, and one of the few waterfalls near Chiang Mai you can hear before you see. The spray reaches well past the viewing platform on a windy day — bring a hat you don't mind getting wet, or just accept it and enjoy the cool-down.

💡Good to know: the rocks near the base get slippery — the marked platform is the safer view, not the missed opportunity.
Waterfall · Sri Lanna National Park

Bua Tong "Sticky" Waterfall

The trick with Bua Tong is that you don't walk around it — you walk up it. The limestone underfoot has an unusually high mineral grip, so bare feet stick to the wet rock instead of sliding, and the whole waterfall turns into a climbable staircase. It looks precarious in photos and feels much safer in person.

💡Good to know: go barefoot or in water shoes — socks and regular sneakers lose all the grip that makes this place work.
Wildlife · no-riding camp

Elephant Care Experience

A morning with a small herd — feeding, mixing fruit, walking alongside them, and for most people, a first time standing close enough to actually see how enormous they are. The camps your driver uses are no-riding: the interaction is care and observation, not a saddle.

💡Good to know: bring a change of clothes — bath time with the herd is rarely a dry activity.
Nature · south of the city

Ob Khan National Park

Quieter than the Doi Inthanon route and usually skipped by the tour vans, which is most of its appeal. A hanging bamboo bridge crosses the Mae Wang river gorge, with hiking trails, small waterfalls, and rock formations on either side. Good for a half-day that doesn't feel like everyone else's half-day.

💡Good to know: the bamboo bridge sways more than it looks like it should — it's fine, just don't stop in the middle to take photos while someone's crossing behind you.
Adventure · half-day add-on

Shooting Range Day

A popular add-on for groups who want one active, slightly adrenaline stop in the day. Supervised the whole way through, with safety gear provided and eye and ear protection non-negotiable. You keep the paper target — most people do.

💡Good to know: closed-toe shoes are required, and long trousers are more comfortable than shorts around the brass.
Coffee · mountain-road stop

Phoenix Coffee Roastery

Northern Thailand's highlands grow genuinely good Arabica, and small roasteries along the mountain roads are where most of it gets brewed properly. Phoenix is a regular stop between routes — a chance to get out of the car, stretch, and drink something better than what's in most hotel lobbies.

💡Good to know: ask what's freshly roasted that week — the small-batch stuff changes, and it's usually worth asking rather than defaulting to the house blend.
